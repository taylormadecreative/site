What one take makes

Every example here is real, from two takes recorded with the app: a one-minute solo promo, and the Auntie PB episode above. Its camera footage was color graded by hand afterward; the app doesn't grade.

01 Reels, TikTok and ShortsReels, made for you.

Press M after a great moment and, when you stop, it's a 9:16 clip with captions at social loudness. Recording yourself? A couple of clicks turn the whole take into a reel with the mistakes and long pauses cut out, three covers to pick from and a caption to post.

Captions on clips and reels need macOS 26

02 Spotify and Apple PodcastsYour podcast, ready to upload.

Every episode comes with podcast audio: both mics mixed, whoever isn't talking turned down, and leveled to the podcast standard. Drag it into your podcast host, like Spotify for Creators, and paste in the title and description Apple Intelligence drafted. Uploading is always up to you. Nothing posts by itself.

Install the beta

Free while it's in beta. Five steps, about two minutes.

  1. 01

    Download

    Click Download for Mac and open TaylormadePodcast.dmg from your Downloads.

  2. 02

    Drag it in

    Drag Taylormade Podcast into your Applications folder.

  3. 03

    Open it

    Open it from Applications. Your Mac asks if you're sure, because it came from the internet: click Open. Then allow the camera and microphone, local network if you'll use iPhones as cameras, and speech recognition (for transcripts) if it asks.

  4. 04

    Try it

    No gear handy? Turn on Practice mode and try everything with pretend cameras and voices. A step-by-step guide opens the first time.

  5. 05

    Add an iPhone

    Optional. Any iPhone works as a camera through Continuity Camera. With Taylormade Camera 2.1 (coming soon to the App Store), open Setup, click Add an iPhone, and scan the code from the app's Podcast tab.

If something goes wrong

My Mac says it can't open the app

Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to Taylormade Podcast.

I clicked Don't Allow for the camera, mic, local network or speech recognition

Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, then Camera, Microphone, Local Network or Speech Recognition, and turn Taylormade Podcast on. Quit and reopen the app.

My camera doesn't show up

In the app, open Setup and click Add a camera. It has the steps for iPhones, webcams, and cameras over USB or HDMI, with menu paths for Canon, Fujifilm, Nikon, Panasonic and Sony.

It can't find my iPhones

Put the Mac and the iPhones on the same Wi-Fi (not a guest network). On the Mac, allow Local Network for Taylormade Podcast (System Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network). On the iPhone, open Settings › Taylormade Camera and turn on Local Network.

Works with the gear you have

Up to four cameras and two mics, any mix.

iPhones

Any iPhone works as a wireless camera through Continuity Camera. The free Taylormade Camera app (version 2.1, coming soon to the App Store) adds more: each iPhone records its own full-quality copy and shows that person their own prompter.

Cameras

Canon, Fujifilm, Nikon, Panasonic, Sony and more, over a USB cable (if the camera has a webcam setting) or HDMI through a capture card. Shooting log? The app converts it to normal color for the show and clips. That's a conversion, not a color grade: the camera files stay as shot.

Webcams

Plug in and pick it. The MacBook camera works too.

Mics

Up to two USB mics or wireless mic receivers, one per person. The Mac's own mic records a spare in the background.

Questions

The privacy page lists everything the app does and doesn't do.

Is Taylormade Podcast free?

Yes, it's free while it's in beta. Everything you record is saved on your Mac, so it's yours to keep.

What does beta mean?

It works, and I'm still fixing things. Try Practice mode first. For a show you can't record again, keep a backup recorder running.

Do I need the Taylormade Camera iPhone app?

No. Any camera you can connect to a Mac works. Any iPhone also works as a wireless camera through Continuity Camera. The free Taylormade Camera app (version 2.1, coming soon to the App Store) adds more: each iPhone keeps a full-quality copy, shows its person a prompter, and lets you start, stop and mark clips from the phone, even by saying “start recording.”

What Mac do I need?

macOS 15 Sequoia or later, on an Apple silicon or Intel Mac. Transcripts, and the captions on clips and reels, need macOS 26. The AI drafts (titles, show notes, clip captions and suggested clips) also need Apple Intelligence turned on, on a Mac that supports it.

Where do my recordings go?

To the Movies folder on your Mac (Movies › Taylormade Podcast), or any folder you pick. Your recordings are never uploaded.

How do I send feedback?

In the app, choose Help › Send Beta Feedback. It opens an email with your Mac and camera details already filled in. Or email taylormademd@gmail.com.

How do I get updates?

When a new beta is out, a gold Update button shows up at the top of the app. Click it to download the new beta, quit the app, then open the download and drag it into Applications. Choose Replace when your Mac asks. Your recordings and settings stay put.

Is there a Windows version?

No. Taylormade Podcast is a Mac app.
