Why do some headshots cost $400+?

WHAT CHANGES THE PRICE

The session fee is only part of the price. Photography Shark found that add-ons routinely raise the base price by 40 to 60 percent. These are the things that move it.

  1. Retouched images. Most quotes include a set number of retouched finals, and every extra image costs more. Compare prices per finished photo, not per session.
  2. Looks and outfits. One outfit against one background is the standard short session. Each extra look adds time.
  3. Session length. A 30-minute session and a two-hour session are different products, even when both are called "headshots."
  4. Hair and makeup. A stylist on set is usually a separate line item.
  5. Location. Coming to you, at an office or on location, adds travel and setup.
  6. Usage rights. Photos for ads or commercial campaigns can cost more than photos for LinkedIn or a casting profile.

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING
What should I ask before booking a headshot photographer?

How many retouched images are included, how many looks or outfits you get, how long the session is, whether you see the full gallery and choose your own picks, when the finals arrive, and whether hair and makeup cost extra. Those answers explain most of the price difference between two quotes.

Can I get more than one look or a longer session?

Yes. The $150 session is one look. For multiple looks or a longer session, send an inquiry through the booking page and you'll get a quote within one business day.

Do you photograph actors and performers?

Yes. The same session works for actor headshots, dance and performing arts, and personal branding. If you're submitting to agencies and need casting digitals instead, those are a separate $100 session.
